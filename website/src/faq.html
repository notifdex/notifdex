Frequently asked questions

Notifdex works differently from cloud aggregators and unified inboxes—it reads the notification banners macOS already shows you. That unlocks apps that block APIs, and it comes with a few real-world nuances worth knowing up front.

Does Notifdex connect to my apps or read my full message history?

No. Notifdex never connects to app APIs, never asks for your passwords, and never reads inside Messages, WhatsApp, Gmail, or any other app’s database. It only records the text macOS exposes on notification banners through Accessibility—including iPhone alerts forwarded to your Mac.

That trade-off is deliberate: it’s why Notifdex works across locked-down apps with no logins or cloud servers. It also means Notifdex only sees what appears in the banner—long messages may be truncated by macOS, image or file attachments aren’t pulled, and messages that never produced a visible notification on your Mac aren’t captured.

What if two people I know have the exact same name?

Notification banners usually show a sender’s display name—like “Alex Chen” or “David”—without a phone number, email address, or permanent ID. If two people share a name in the same app, raw banners alone can’t tell them apart.

Because of this, Notifdex never creates or merges people automatically by display name. You link alerts to a person explicitly, and if you turn on Remember this match for an app and title and later link that same name to someone else, Notifdex pauses the rule automatically instead of misfiling future alerts. For lookalike names, keeping them in separate workspaces (like Work and Personal) and checking the preview before linking keeps things clean.

Can Notifdex tell which account received an alert (like personal vs. work Gmail)?

Not reliably yet. When you’re signed into multiple accounts inside one app—such as two Gmail inboxes or multiple X accounts—macOS notification banners often don’t include which receiving account triggered the alert.

Rather than guessing, Notifdex saves what macOS exposes and lets you assign alerts to workspaces manually. We’re investigating reliable receiving-account attribution in #3 and #9.

What happens when my Mac is asleep, locked, or in Focus mode?

If your Mac is asleep or Focus suppresses a banner, macOS doesn’t display that alert while it arrives. When your Mac wakes, Recovery can scan notifications still sitting unread in Notification Center—and any time window where Notifdex wasn’t watching is logged honestly as a coverage gap instead of pretending nothing was missed.

Alerts you already read and cleared on your iPhone while your Mac was asleep won’t still be in Notification Center, so those stay unknown. See the reliability notes for the full breakdown.

What do I need for forwarded iPhone notifications to work?

For Mac-only alerts, any Mac on macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later works. For forwarded iPhone alerts, Apple requires:

  • An Apple Silicon or T2 Mac on macOS 15 (Sequoia) or later, and an iPhone on iOS 18 or later signed into the same Apple Account (note: Apple currently restricts iPhone Mirroring in the EU).
  • iPhone Mirroring paired at least once, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on.
  • Allow notifications from iPhone enabled in macOS System Settings → Notifications—including each individual iPhone app toggled on inside that list.
  • Show Previews set to Always or When Unlocked (if set to Never, macOS hides the sender and message text behind a generic placeholder).
  • The matching app enabled under App Center → Apps inside Notifdex (X and Messages start enabled by default).

Why does Notifdex ask for Accessibility access, and where is my data stored?

macOS doesn’t offer a public Notification Center history API, so Accessibility is the only local way for a Mac app to read incoming notification banners without requiring your app logins. Notifdex only reads alerts from the apps you turn on.

Everything is stored locally in one SQLite database at ~/Library/Application Support/Notifdex/capture.db with private file permissions (protected at rest by macOS FileVault). Nothing is uploaded anywhere. Signed Homebrew upgrades (brew update && brew upgrade --cask notifdex) keep the same signing identity so your Accessibility grant carries across updates.

Why does “Open in …” open the app instead of the exact message?

Notification banners don’t expose a deep link or conversation ID for the underlying thread, and Notifdex intentionally avoids putting captured notification text into URLs. Clicking Open in Messages, Open in Gmail, or Open in WhatsApp takes you straight to the app or inbox so you can reply there safely.
