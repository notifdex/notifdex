Notifications were built to interrupt you, not to help you remember.

Most notification tools hit a wall: webhook apps can’t touch Messages or WhatsApp, iOS blocks apps from reading each other’s alerts, and unified inboxes ask for passwords to every account you own.

Your Mac already receives your desktop and forwarded iPhone notifications. Notifdex reads them locally on macOS—so it works with any app that shows a banner, with no APIs, no logins, and no subscription.

One place to look back

Search across Messages, WhatsApp, Gmail, X, Slack, Discord, LinkedIn, and any other app you pick. Flag what needs a reply, jump back into the original app, or mute recurring noise with a rule you can undo.

Organized around people

Link someone’s texts, WhatsApp messages, and emails to one profile, across separate workspaces for work, family, or projects. You decide who’s who—Notifdex never guesses or merges people by name.

Room to follow through

Attach a due date to any notification and work through your list in Today. Notes you keep on a person stay in sync with your personal notebook.

Private, and honest about its limits

Everything stays in one local SQLite file on your Mac. Because Notifdex only sees what macOS shows, it logs when your Mac was asleep or a preview was hidden instead of pretending nothing was missed.

Try it on your Mac

Notifdex builds from source in about a minute with Apple’s Command Line Tools. Signed public downloads are on the way (#20, #22).

  1. For iPhone alerts, turn on Allow notifications from iPhone in macOS System Settings.
  2. Open App Center from your menu bar and grant Accessibility access.
  3. Choose which apps to keep under Apps. Closing the window leaves Notifdex running in the menu bar.
